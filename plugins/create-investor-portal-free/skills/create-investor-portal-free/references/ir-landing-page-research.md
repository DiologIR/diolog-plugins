# ASX issuer IR landing page — what search- and LLM-referred investors actually need

## Executive Summary

- **(High Confidence)** ASX Listing Rule 15.7 is the hard architectural constraint, not a footnote: an entity "must not release information that is for release to the market to any person until it has given the information to ASX and has received an acknowledgement that ASX has released the information to the market" [asx.com.au](https://www.asx.com.au/documents/rules/Chapter15.pdf). Any auto-publishing announcement feed, price widget, or "latest news" module on the hub must be gated on the MAP release acknowledgement — an ASX Online lodgement status message does not count [asx.com.au](https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/rules-guidance-notes-waivers/asx-listing-rules/guidance-notes/gn14-asx-market-announcements-platform.pdf).

- **(High Confidence)** The only near-mandatory *page* on an Australian issuer site is the corporate governance page. Listing Rule 4.10.3 permits the corporate governance statement to live at a URL instead of in the annual report, that URL is lodged with ASX under LR 4.7.4, and Guidance Note 9 directs that website governance disclosures be "clearly presented and centrally located on, or accessible from, a 'corporate governance' landing page" with an intuitive navigation link [asx.com.au](https://www.asx.com.au/documents/rules/gn09_disclosure_corporate_governance_practices.pdf). That makes the governance link non-negotiable furniture and the URL effectively permanent.

- **(Medium Confidence)** Investors arriving cold want narrative before numbers. NN/g's IR study (63 participants, 94 sites, US/UK/HK, five methods including eyetracking) found investors "want more than just financial data — they want the company's story and business vision," and recommends prioritising financial content into high/medium/low tiers rather than dumping it [nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/investor-relations-ir-corporate-websites/).

- **(Medium Confidence)** For LLM citability, crawlable factual density beats markup. The KDD 2024 GEO paper (GEO-bench, ~10,000 queries, nine datasets) reports 30–40% gains on position-adjusted word count from adding statistics, quotations and source citations [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735). No study links schema.org markup to citation rate, and Google publishes no rich result derived from `Corporation`/`tickerSymbol` [schema.org](https://schema.org/tickerSymbol).

- **(High Confidence)** Do not build the page around `llms.txt` or `FAQPage` schema. Google's John Mueller has confirmed no Search system reads `llms.txt`, and a ~300,000-domain test found no significant correlation with AI citation frequency `UNVERIFIED (unusable citation URL)`. FAQ rich results were restricted to government/health sites in August 2023 [developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes) and removed entirely on 7 May 2026 [developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage).

- **(Low Confidence)** There is no independent evidence that any specific IR-page element drives shareholder-register growth. Every register-growth number in the Australian market traces to vendor self-published case studies [investorhub.com](https://investorhub.com/au-case-studies). This is the weakest leg of the brief and should not be used to justify a section's inclusion.

- **(Medium Confidence)** Two capability gaps are unserved by every vendor in the AU set: (1) an HTML-first, per-announcement page with a stable URL and structured data — announcements are universally PDF blobs behind a registry or third-party widget; (2) a canonical, dated, machine-readable "company facts" block (shares on issue, cash at bank as at date, board, registry) that an answer engine can lift without parsing an Appendix 4C PDF.

- **(High Confidence)** Forward-looking content on the page is a legal surface, not a marketing one. Section 769C of the Corporations Act deems a representation about a future matter misleading if made without reasonable grounds; ASIC restates this in RG 280 for sustainability claims [download.asic.gov.au](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/j4rhwyiz/rg280-published-31-march-2025.pdf) and applies it hard to mining/resources production targets and DCF valuations in INFO 214, where "compliance with the ASX Listing Rules is not enough" [asic.gov.au](https://asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/takeovers/forward-looking-statements/mining-and-resources-forward-looking-statements/).

## Detailed Findings

### What do retail and professional investors actually need from an ASX-listed issuer's public IR landing page when they arrive from a search engine or an LLM assistant?

#### (a) The first 30 seconds

The direct evidence base here is thinner than the question implies. NN/g is the only rigorous usability study of IR sections I could verify: 63 participants across investors, financial analysts/advisors and business journalists, 94 websites, US/UK/Hong Kong, using one-on-one testing, eyetracking, card sorting, expert review and interviews; 206 pages, 103 design recommendations [nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/investor-relations-ir-corporate-websites/). Its headline finding is that investors want the company's story and business vision alongside the financial data, and that IR content must be tiered because the audience spans entry-level retail through professional analysts and journalists [nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/investor-relations-ir-corporate-websites/). Its topic list — company information, stock quotes, stock charts, financials in HTML *and* PDF, calendar/events, email alerts, webcasts, slide presentations, contacting IR — is the closest thing to a validated section inventory [nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/investor-relations-ir-corporate-websites/).

<MISSING_DATA>[Sought: a study ranking what investors look at first on an IR page, or eyetracking fixation order. The NN/g PDF exceeded the fetch size limit (10MB) and the summary page lists topics, not guidelines. Needed: the 3rd-edition PDF read locally, or an IR Impact / Rivel benchmark with fixation or click-order data.]</MISSING_DATA>

The Australian demand-side context: ASX's *Australian Investor Study 2023* (Investment Trends, online survey, n=5,519 nationally representative adults, fielded November 2022) reports 7.7m Australians holding an exchange-listed asset, 22% having started within the prior two years, and — most relevant here — that the single biggest challenge respondents named was **knowing which source to trust** [asx.com.au](https://www.asx.com.au/investors/investment-tools-and-resources/australian-investor-study). A large, recently-arrived, trust-anxious retail cohort is exactly the traffic an LLM referral delivers.

<INFERENCE from="NN/g's finding that investors want story+vision and tiered financial content; the ASX study's 'knowing which source to trust' as the top reported challenge; and the LR 15.7 requirement that all market-sensitive content originate from MAP">A cold visitor's first-30-second questions are, in order: (1) what does this company actually do, in one sentence, in plain language; (2) is this the official company source or a third-party aggregator; (3) what stage is it at — revenue, pre-revenue, cash position, and as at when; (4) what is the most recent thing that happened and when; (5) what happens next and when; (6) who is accountable. Price and chart matter less than the design convention suggests, because the referring surface (search result, LLM answer, broker app) has usually already supplied the price. The differentiating content an issuer page uniquely owns is the dated, sourced fact set and the announcement stream — not the quote widget.</INFERENCE>

<CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>[NN/g treats stock quotes and charts as core IR content requiring their own guidelines [nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/investor-relations-ir-corporate-websites/), but the report predates both the brokerage-app era and LLM referral. No source I found tests whether a quote widget is still load-bearing on an issuer page in 2026. The inference above departs from NN/g on this point and is Low Confidence.]</CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>

#### (b) What makes an issuer IR page eligible for LLM citation

**Crawlability is the gate, and it is the thing most likely to be broken.** Multiple analyses converge on accessibility preceding every other factor [searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/guide/optimize-for-ai-crawlers). A 1M+ citation analysis across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews (Jan–Feb 2026) reports 73% of sites carry technical barriers blocking AI crawler access [otterly.ai](https://otterly.ai/blog/the-ai-citations-report-2026/) `[SECONDARY: vendor]`. Blocks come from firewall and bot-management rules as often as from `robots.txt` [searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/guide/optimize-for-ai-crawlers). Academic work confirms rapid escalation of AI-crawler blocking among reputable publishers — from 23% (Sept 2023) to ~60% (May 2025) [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10315).

The user agents that matter for the four referring engines: `GPTBot` (ChatGPT), `PerplexityBot`, `ClaudeBot`, and `Google-Extended` (Gemini/AI Overviews); blocking one removes the domain from that engine's citation pool [searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/guide/optimize-for-ai-crawlers).

**Factual density is the strongest evidenced content lever, and it is contested.** Aggarwal et al., "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization," KDD 2024 (DOI 10.1145/3637528.3671900) [dblp.dagstuhl.de](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/rec/conf/kdd/AggarwalMRKND24.html), evaluated on GEO-bench (~10,000 queries, nine datasets, top-5 Google sources per query, GPT-3.5-turbo synthesis), reports that adding statistics, quotations from relevant sources, and cited sources yields 30–40% gains on position-adjusted word count and 15–30% on subjective impression [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735).

<CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>[C-SEO Bench (Puerto et al., 2025) reportedly found most GEO tactics do not help and some hurt `UNVERIFIED (unusable citation URL)`. Secondary sources also disagree on the per-tactic figures (one reports Cite Sources +40%/Statistics +37%/Quotations +22%; another reports Quotations 40%/Statistics 34%/Citations 27%) and the numbers differ between arXiv v1, v2 and the KDD camera-ready. Use the 30–40% band, not any single per-tactic figure.]</CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE>

**Structured data is an entity signal, not a citation lever.** `tickerSymbol` exists only on the `Corporation` subtype and is the sole markup difference from `Organization` [schema.org](https://schema.org/tickerSymbol). Google documents no rich result from it and reports no errors for it in Search Console. Schema.org specifies the value as exchange and instrument separated by a space, using MIC codes — but published examples in the wild use a colon (`"NASDAQ:AAPL"`) [schema.org](https://schema.org/tickerSymbol). For an ASX issuer that means `XASX ABC` per spec.

<INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE>[No controlled study ties schema.org markup to LLM citation rate. The closest academic work argues RAG systems *discard* embedded JSON-LD and treats exploiting it as an open problem, which is an argument that markup *should* help, not evidence that it does [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10700v1). Emit `Corporation` markup because it is cheap and correct, not because it is evidenced to win citations.]</INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE>

**Do not build for `llms.txt` or `FAQPage`.** Both are dead ends as of 2026. On `llms.txt`: 62,100 logged AI bot visits over 90 days produced 84 hits on the file — 0.1% [otterly.ai](https://otterly.ai/blog/the-ai-citations-report-2026/) `[SECONDARY: vendor]`; among the 50 most AI-cited domains, one had the file `UNVERIFIED (unusable citation URL)`. On `FAQPage`: restricted to authoritative government and health sites in August 2023 [developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes), then removed entirely 7 May 2026 with reporting retired through June–August 2026 [developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage). The markup remains valid schema and causes no harm; it just buys nothing.

**Freshness matters disproportionately in finance.** Industries with rapidly changing information, financial services named specifically, show extreme recency bias in AI citations; finance also shows the highest citation volatility across verticals [otterly.ai](https://otterly.ai/blog/the-ai-citations-report-2026/) `[SECONDARY: vendor]`. Practical consequence: visible, machine-readable `dateModified` and per-item publication dates on every fact and announcement.

**Engine-specific citation pools barely overlap.** Profound ran 100,000 prompts through ChatGPT and Perplexity across a 680-million-citation dataset and found only 11.0% of cited domains appeared in both; 37.4% ChatGPT-only, 51.6% Perplexity-only [tryprofound.com](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns) `[SECONDARY: vendor]`. An older academic audit of 672 queries across two systems found 3,448 citations spanning 355 unique domains with 26% domain overlap [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.14034).

<INFERENCE from="the 11%/26% cross-engine overlap figures; the finding that community platforms take ~52.5% of citations vs 47.5% for brand domains; and the finance-vertical recency bias">An issuer page will not win citation share by outranking Reddit, HotCopper or aggregators on general company queries. It wins on the narrow class of queries where the issuer is the only possible primary source — shares on issue as at a date, board composition, cash at bank, the text of a specific announcement, AGM date and venue, registry contact. The page should be engineered to be unambiguously the authoritative primary for those, and should not attempt to compete on interpretive or comparative content.</INFERENCE>

**Disclaimer furniture.** The forward-looking-statement disclaimer is convention, not rule — and the regulatory record above shows why treating it as a shield is wrong. ### What is the current state, and what is the strongest supporting evidence for it?

The strongest-evidenced state is regulatory: the ASX Listing Rules and ASIC guidance cited above are primary instruments and are unambiguous. The second-strongest is crawler access, where both academic [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10315) and large-N vendor telemetry [otterly.ai](https://otterly.ai/blog/the-ai-citations-report-2026/) agree that access is the binding constraint and that blocking is widespread and rising.

### What are the contrasting viewpoints or competing evidence?

Three live disputes bear on the design:

1. **Does content optimisation actually lift AI citation?** GEO (KDD 2024) says 30–40% [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735); C-SEO Bench reportedly finds most tactics neutral-to-harmful `UNVERIFIED (unusable citation URL)`. Compounding this, most academic GEO work is evaluated on static corpora and synthetic catalogues rather than deployed answer engines, so offline results may not transfer.

2. **Brand-owned vs earned/community content.** Community platforms take 52.5% of citations against 47.5% for brand domains [otterly.ai](https://otterly.ai/blog/the-ai-citations-report-2026/) `[SECONDARY: vendor]`, and a reported September 2025 GEO study found systematic bias toward earned media over brand-owned content `UNVERIFIED (unusable citation URL)`. That is an argument against expecting the issuer hub to dominate, and for scoping it to the queries where it is the only primary.

3. **Structured data: signal or discarded metadata?** Vendor guidance treats markup as a citation lever; the one academic treatment says RAG systems routinely discard the JSON-LD sites embed [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10700v1). Both agree the markup is currently underexploited; they disagree on whether that will change.

### What changed recently, and what is the trajectory?

- **August 2023 → May 2026:** FAQ rich results narrowed to government/health [developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes) then removed outright on 7 May 2026, with the Rich Results Test and Search Console reporting retired through June and August 2026 [developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage). June 2025 retired seven further types. Trajectory: Google is contracting the set of markup types that produce visible results, which weakens the case for markup-driven design.
- **Sept 2023 → May 2025:** AI-crawler blocking among reputable sites rose from 23% to ~60% [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10315). Trajectory: the citation pool is thinning, which raises the value of *not* blocking.
- **March 2025:** ASIC published RG 280 on sustainability reporting, extending the s769C reasonable-grounds framing to climate and sustainability representations [download.asic.gov.au](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/j4rhwyiz/rg280-published-31-march-2025.pdf). Trajectory: more of an IR page's narrative content falls inside the forward-looking-statement perimeter, not less.
- **Retail composition:** 52% of Australian adults held investments beyond home and super, up from 45% in 2020; 22% of investors started within two years of the survey [asx.com.au](https://www.asx.com.au/investors/investment-tools-and-resources/australian-investor-study). Trajectory: the cold-arrival, low-context audience is growing.

**Gap 1 — HTML-first per-announcement pages.** No vendor in the AU set ships a per-announcement HTML page with a stable canonical URL, visible publication datetime, and `NewsArticle`-class structured data. ## Knowledge Gaps

**Cause: source exists but was not retrievable.**
- The NN/g 3rd-edition PDF (206 pages, 103 recommendations, 157 screenshots) exceeded the 10MB fetch limit. Its actual guidelines — including anything on what investors look for first, stock information, and trust — remain unread. This is the single most valuable missing document for question (a).
- ASX Guidance Note 8's specific treatment of company websites and social media (release order) was not directly verified; the principle is well attested in commentary but I did not read GN8's text.

**Cause: the research does not exist.**
- No study links schema.org markup to LLM citation rate.
- No study isolates financial or issuer domains' AI Overviews / answer-engine citation share.
- No independent study links any IR-page element to shareholder-register growth in any market, let alone Australia.
- No IR-specific email-alert subscription conversion benchmark is public.
- No source ranks what investors look at first on an IR page in the LLM-referral era.

**Cause: contested or version-unstable.**
- GEO per-tactic figures differ across arXiv v1/v2/camera-ready and are disputed by C-SEO Bench, whose primary source I could not retrieve.
- `tickerSymbol` serialisation: schema.org specifies space-separated MIC + instrument; deployed examples use colon-separated. Google parses neither into a documented feature, so the conflict is currently inconsequential but unresolved.

**Cause: not attempted in scope.**
- No empirical crawl of ASX issuer IR pages to measure PDF-only / client-side-rendered announcement archives. Gap 1 rests on inference, not measurement.
- ASX market-data licence terms for issuer-hosted price display.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Read the NN/g 3rd-edition PDF locally** (download rather than fetch; it exceeds the 10MB tool limit). Rationale: it is the only rigorous, multi-method, multi-audience usability evidence for IR sections in existence, and its 103 guidelines are the closest thing to a validated section inventory. Every (a)-side decision is currently resting on the report's summary page rather than its contents.

2. **Crawl 50–100 ASX issuer IR pages twice — raw HTML and rendered DOM — and diff.** Rationale: converts Gap 1 from inference to measurement. Record for each: whether announcements are server-rendered HTML, whether per-announcement stable URLs exist, whether the archive is a third-party iframe/widget, `robots.txt` treatment of `GPTBot`/`PerplexityBot`/`ClaudeBot`/`Google-Extended`, and presence of `Corporation` markup. This produces the competitive baseline the design should beat and is cheap.

3. **Run a controlled answer-engine citation test on the fact classes where the issuer is the only primary source.** Query ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews for "how many shares does [ASX:XYZ] have on issue", "when is [ASX:XYZ]'s AGM", "who is on [ASX:XYZ]'s board", across ~30 issuers; record which domain is cited. Rationale: the 11% cross-engine overlap figure [tryprofound.com](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns) means engine-specific behaviour must be measured, not assumed, and this directly tests the Gap-2 thesis that aggregators are winning citations on issuer-authored facts.

4. **Obtain the ASX market-data licence terms and confirm whether an issuer may display delayed price with what attribution.** Rationale: this is a binary include/exclude decision for a hero price widget and is currently unresolved; the registry 20-minute-delay convention [mpms.mufg.com](https://www.mpms.mufg.com/en/for-individuals/au/shareholders/faq/) suggests a constraint exists but does not establish its terms for issuers.

5. **Instrument the alert-subscription funnel from launch and publish the resulting benchmark.** Rationale: the absence of any IR-specific conversion benchmark is a genuine hole in the public record, not just in this brief. Capture segment-level opt-in (announcements / financials / events), the double-opt-in completion rate separately from the form-submit rate, and referral source (search vs LLM vs direct). Twelve months of this data would be the first defensible answer to question (c) and is a durable asset regardless of the design decision it was collected to inform.
