## Batch summary

**Era distribution (n=50):** big-sur 17 · skeuomorphic 15 · flat-brand 8 · liquid-glass 5 · custom-web 5

**Dominant palette families:** Blue dominates overwhelmingly — roughly 20 icons use a cobalt/azure gradient ground (#2060E0–#4AA8F8 band), usually with a white or frosted glyph. Second family: violet/indigo (Cubox, Expenses, VinylPod, Retcon, WhisperType, Magic). Third: warm accents (amber/orange) used almost exclusively as single accents or skeuomorphic material color, rarely as grounds (Weather mini and Stitcher being border exceptions). Monochrome black/white is a deliberate minority stance (Pieoneer, OneClock, Hex Fiend, Artykul). Accent discipline is generally good: the strongest icons hold to one hue family plus at most one accent.

**Recurring glyph devices:** (1) tool-breaking-the-frame diagonals — pen/marker/knife/needle/crowbar appear in 6 icons (Precircuiter, Retcon, Posterino, Stitcher, Hex Fiend, Magic); (2) blueprint/grid grounds with keyline insets (TOTP, Swiftly, Screenshot-in-Phone-Frame, LaunchBuddy, MacFamilyTree, Verba) — the Apple Icon Composer template idiom; (3) document/receipt/card sheets with soft gel extrusion (Accountit, Expenses, VoicePen); (4) flip-clock and vinyl-record physical objects; (5) 3D mascots/characters (Bluebird, Gapplin, Cubox, Angry Birds); (6) literal devices as full-bleed silhouettes (Framous, Lickable, OneClock).

**Material trends / Liquid Glass tells:** The 2025 icons increasingly show the pre-macOS-26 glass vocabulary: frosted translucent glyphs on blueprint-gridded blue grounds (TOTP shield, Swiftly briefcase, the duplicate glass phone in Screenshot-in-Phone-Frame), layered translucency with rim highlights (WhisperType's waveform lobes, LaunchBuddy's frosted fins), and blur-as-subject (DeskMat). Gel/clay soft extrusion remains the workhorse material for indie big-sur icons (thick white cards with tinted side faces, bottom-weighted gradients, soft contact shadows). True photoreal PBR rendering persists in a healthy skeuomorphic wing (PixelGriddle, Hyperspace, Bluebird, Fast Collections) — notably always full-bleed objects rather than glyphs-on-grounds. Near-white grounds are rarer here than expected; saturated blue gradients are still the default ground of this period.

**A-grade standouts (8):**
- **Photomator** — the pro-app archetype perfected: one saturated lens-orb on a quiet neutral ground, flawless gradient and gloss control.
- **Hex Fiend** — crowbar-through-hex-dump; a tool metaphor so exact the icon needs no color at all.
- **Bluebird** — character-render craft (subsurface gel, worn leather goggles) at feature-film quality while keeping a clean silhouette.
- **PixelGriddle** — pun made physical (pixel grid = waffle griddle) with genuinely tactile cast-iron and batter textures.
- **WhisperType** — layered translucent waveform on ruled paper; the clearest home-grown Liquid Glass sensibility in the batch.
- Also cited: **Hyperspace** (cinematic drive-at-warp), **Sleeve** (two-tone object-within-object discipline), **Tes** (painterly specimen butterfly), **Magic** (grain-texture anti-gloss signature).

**Common failure modes:** (1) readable text/screenshots baked into the icon (MDB ACCDB Viewer's data table, Lickable's literal menu-bar screenshot) — illegible at Dock sizes; (2) photographic grounds (Hydra) that read as thumbnails rather than icons; (3) style stacking — three visual languages in one tile (Countdown Pro's mural + device + rim, Shot Lister's flat board + glossy check); (4) template anonymity — competent Apple-blueprint or gel executions with no ownable mark (TOTP, Swiftly, MiniBlocker); (5) unadapted brand/franchise transplants centered on white (Shazam, Angry Birds).
